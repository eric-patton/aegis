# The Next Stretch: a plan from the research sweep (July 2026)

This document turns the July 2026 research sweep (research/11 Daggerfall, research/12
Kingdom Death: Monster, research/13 procedural-world lessons, plus the earlier 00-10
corpus) and the current roadmap state (through D-129; D-130, the Long Siege, landed the
same day and changes nothing here) into a concrete plan. It is a planning artifact, not
the tracker: `design/roadmap.md` stays the source of truth for what has shipped.
**Adopted as D-131 (2026-07-20):** the plan's items are now tracked as roadmap boxes
(the stead-layer and Phase D sections, plus lines under the existing pillars), its open
questions sit in the decisions.md parking lot, and the corrections found at adoption are
folded in below (B1's camping claim, the generator-freeze claim, D3's audit carrier).

## Decisions made with the user (2026-07-20)

These were asked and answered explicitly; the plan below assumes them.

1. **World scale: multi-region.** Each world grows to a handful of distinct
   regions/settlements over time, keeping valley-level density in each. Not one valley
   forever, and not a Daggerfall-scale sprawl of thin regions.
2. **Stead layer: the facilities version.** Adopt research/12's steps 1-3 and 5:
   scheduled future facts, stead event storylets on the coarse tick, a 5-8 facility
   coin-sink ladder, and the crossing split (facilities reset with the world, knowledge
   carries). Named stead folk (step 4) is deferred, not rejected.
3. **Priorities: all four lanes** (stead layer, breadth, worldgen scale-up, pacing/NG+
   freshness), with the **stead layer first** and worldgen scale-up starting after its
   core lands.
4. **First new region: a market town**, with market, smithy, law and guild texture, and
   its own factions.
5. **NG+ freshness: worlds differ in kind.** Per-world generated rules/twists become a
   long-term pillar, not just growing content pools.

## Guiding principles distilled from the research

The full arguments live in the research files; these are the rules the plan applies.

- **Perceptual uniqueness is the only metric** (research/13, Compton's oatmeal problem).
  Adopt the "fanfic test" as a content gate: an entity or event earns generation effort
  only if a player could tell a story about it. Everything else is texture and should be
  cheap and honest about being texture.
- **Density before area** (research/11, 13). Daggerfall's negative proof: scale without
  queryable meaning reads as empty. Every new region must open at valley density or not
  open yet. Aegis's fact-graph-first architecture is the right bet; regions are added
  one at a time, each fully wired into facts, prices, topics, and templates.
- **Authorship in the parts, generation in the arrangement** (research/13, Spelunky/Qud).
  The authored template and chunk libraries are the game. Budget sessions for growing
  them the way sessions are already budgeted for systems.
- **Consequence beats template count** (research/11). Daggerfall's 227 quest templates
  still produced mad-libs fatigue because outcomes were not recorded and re-surfaced.
  Aegis already lives by this (every fact gets a reader, the D-109/D-128 discipline);
  keep it as the standing answer to repetition complaints, never "add more templates."
- **Something with editorial authority above the tick** (research/13, RimWorld). The
  coarse tick is causal, not dramatic. A pacing layer should eventually shape when
  storylets and events fire, so quiet stretches and pile-ups are chosen, not accidental.
- **The future as visible, mutable data** (research/12). KDM's timeline is its best
  mechanism: scheduled threats the player can see coming, foreshadowed, and sometimes
  cancel. This is the single highest-leverage import for Aegis.
- **Simulation on the world does more replayability work than generation of the world**
  (research/13, Mount & Blade/Kenshi). The coarse tick deserves investment on par with
  worldgen as regions multiply.
- **Freeze the generator for a campaign** (research/13, Minecraft's chunk borders).
  D-165 resolves the former half-measure for 1.0: the save header records generator 1,
  and the seed-plus-journal architecture replays every later crossing in that campaign
  through the same supported entry point. New campaigns use the newest supported
  generator; old ones are retained or rejected explicitly, never silently redealt.
- **Text raises the bar on prose variety** (research/13). TUI dodges the 3D asset-variety
  trap, but skeleton repetition is more visible in prose. Render one fact through many
  surfaces (rumor, epitaph, ledger, song), and audit for repeated skeletons across seeds.

## Phase A: the stead grows (the KDM-derived layer)

Goal: the home stead becomes something the bearer invests in and comes home to, riding
entirely on existing machinery (fact graph, storylets, the coarse tick, vendor benches).

- **A1. Scheduled future facts.** The fact graph learns to hold facts about the future:
  a `due` tick, a foreshadow hook (rumor/notice/talk that fires ahead of time), and
  cancellation conditions. The tick fires them when due. First uses can be modest (a
  hard-winter warning that prices bread; the peddler's announced return with new stock)
  because the machinery is the point: every later lane (town caravans, war escalations,
  twist worlds) will write to this calendar. This is KDM's timeline translated into
  Aegis's own idiom.
- **A2. Stead event storylets on the tick.** A small deck of tick-fired stead events
  beyond the raids: mixed weathers, small fortunes and misfortunes, arrivals on the
  road. Each writes a fact and ships with its narration hook (the standing rule: if the
  player could not perceive it, it does not fire), and several should foreshadow via A1
  so coming home to news becomes a loop. Keep the deck small and consequence-dense
  rather than wide and decorative (the fanfic test applies to events too).
- **A3. The facility ladder.** Five to eight stead facilities as an aspirational coin
  sink (the roadmap's open D-025/D-036 box): e.g. the palisade (raids blunted), the
  watchtower (the watch cheaper or foresighted, feeding A1 foreshadowing), the stillroom
  extension (a deeper remedy), a smithy bench (repair at home, a smithing seed), a
  granary (the stores axis deepened). Each tier modifies systems that already exist
  (raids, larder, watch, benches); no facility that only unlocks itself. Priced above
  current means so the ladder stays aspirational; built things are perceivable deeds and
  may feed regard, but capped at a one-time acknowledgment per facility, never per-tier
  or recurring: the regard doctrine is perceivable deeds, and a coin-priced ladder that
  pays regard repeatedly is a coin-to-reputation channel (the D-109 discipline says
  restitution never turns a profit; purchases should not either).
- **A4. The crossing split.** Facilities and stead state reset with the world (they are
  the World bucket); recipes, lessons, and knowledge keep crossing as they already do. A
  small legacy echo is worth considering (the next world's stead remembering a builder in
  its founding talk, via the existing mythology/silence machinery), but keep it light.
- **A5 (deferred). Named stead folk.** A fixed per-world cast with rare, authored deaths
  and persistent graves. Deferred because guests already carry mortal stakes and the
  facility layer should land first; revisit once A1-A3 are live and the raids can
  threaten something with a name. If adopted, keep it "lite": no population simulation,
  no birth rolls (research/12's explicit avoid-list).

Avoid throughout (from research/12): an endeavor-style second action currency (coin and
turns are the currencies), KDM's random tech-draw (facilities are a visible ladder),
mandatory upkeep bookkeeping beyond what the watch already does.

## Phase B: the world widens (multi-region, the Daggerfall lane)

Goal: a second region at full density, and the road between as play.

- **B1. The road.** Travel-as-play machinery before the destination: the overworld grows
  a bounded second area reached by a real journey (supplies, weather exposure, camp
  rests, encounter sites seeded along the way). Daggerfall's warning applies: never ship
  distance that is only distance. The beasts and saddlebags already give this lane many
  of its verbs; camping does not exist yet (it is an open wilderness-living box, D-006)
  and lands as part of B1, not before it.
- **B2. The market town.** The first true town, built the Daggerfall way done right:
  authored chunks (a market row, a guildhall, a court, lanes) stitched per seed, at
  valley density of meaning. It brings its own factions (a guild, a law), its own
  ledgers on the bearer, and the roadmap's town-life breadth: Commerce as a skill, real
  shops with fact-derived prices, smithing lessons (the Smithing skill's seed), later
  tournaments and property (a town room as a facility-ladder rung that lives away from
  home). The law and reputation web should be surfaced, not hidden (research/11's
  biggest missed opportunity: Daggerfall built the deep web and never showed it).
- **B3. Region machinery.** What B1/B2 force into existence, done once and kept general:
  region entities and name pools (the open D-049 box), per-region price spreads (the
  peddler and bench spreads already prototype this), caravan/arbitrage between stead and
  town (the D-025 box, and the peddler's stock-growth deferral), and faction relations
  that cross regions (the town's law hearing about the valley's named thief only as fast
  as news travels).
- **B4. Later regions.** After the town proves the machinery: a wild frontier region
  (new biome, new kinds, wilderness-living depth), then others. One at a time, each at
  full density, is the standing rule.

## Phase C: breadth, interleaved

The open skills and activities, sequenced by what unblocks them rather than as a block:

- **With Phase A:** Smithing's first seed (the stead smithy bench), weather/seasons v1
  (A2's events want weather anyway, and the roadmap already tracks it).
- **With Phase B:** Commerce (the town market), Smithing proper (the town smith's
  lessons), Persuasion (town talk and court), tournaments (the town's set-piece, reusing
  combat's duel-shaped verbs), property (a facility-ladder rung in town).
- **Anytime:** literacy and Lore (books gating recipes/techniques/history; worth landing
  before the town's bookshops and archives so illiterate starts feel it), Stealth (the
  crime family's missing movement half), Athletics.
- **Ongoing:** the catalogs (knacks for new skills, gear, oaths, scars, Legend rungs),
  guest roles and arcs, NPC depth (voices, schedules).

## Phase D: pacing and NG+ freshness (the procgen-lessons lane)

- **D1. The pacing layer.** A storyteller above the coarse tick: not new events, but
  editorial authority over when eligible storylets and tick events fire (spacing after
  hard beats, pressure when the bearer coasts). Start read-only (log what it would have
  done across the sweep seeds) before letting it steer, so its effect is auditable.
- **D2. Worlds that differ in kind.** The per-world twist library: each world past a
  given tier can carry one generated rule that changes how the world works, written into
  history and law (worlds where the dead are traded with, worlds without coin, worlds
  where a faith holds the roads). Twists are authored as templates (the parts), drawn
  and bound per world (the arrangement), and must touch systems, not just flavor. This
  is the long-term answer to players learning the generator across infinite NG+, and it
  should arrive after the town, when there are enough systems for a twist to bend.
- **D3. Prose variety infrastructure.** Fact-keyed prose fragments composed per surface,
  so one fact renders as rumor, epitaph, ledger line, or song without repeating its
  skeleton; plus a repetition audit across seeds. The journey JSON sweep is NOT a
  sufficient carrier: much of the prose is talk-gated and pilot-unexercised by design
  (the D-109/D-111 precedent appears in nearly every changelog entry), so an audit over
  pilot output would systematically miss the surfaces most prone to skeleton repetition.
  The audit wants a generate-then-curate dump instead: a mode that emits every storylet
  and topic surface a world can produce, pilot-visited or not. That dump is also the
  natural seed of D4, so build the two on the same harness.
- **D4. Worldgen evaluation in CI.** Batch-generate worlds and chart expressive-range
  metrics over the fact graph (faction states reached, template mixes, price spreads,
  storylet coverage), the research/13 recommendation that fits the existing
  baseline-sweep discipline almost verbatim. Cheap to start (extend `journey --json`),
  and it hardens every later generator change, so begin it early in Phase B at the
  latest, when the generator starts growing again.

### The 1.0-ready gate

Aegis is 1.0-ready when all nine tranches above are checked off and the release
candidate meets every condition below:

- The full engine sweep is green, including tests, five-seed journey twins, exact sim
  replay, justified baseline drift, and the worldgen purity gate.
- A fresh manual playthrough review is complete, including creation, ordinary play,
  death and recovery, regional travel, the main progression, and repeated crossings.
- No known release-blocking defects remain. Save behavior, help text, and design docs
  match the shipping build.
- Every shipped important fact has a reader, every live conflict has a designed exit,
  and every shipped activity pays at least two of the D-006 outputs.
- Every roadmap item still unchecked or partial is explicitly classified as post-1.0
  or promoted into this gate by a later decision.

Open-ended catalog growth does not block 1.0. More gear, oaths, scars, workings,
templates, regions, line banks, and similar additions continue in a post-1.0 backlog
unless a later decision deliberately promotes one into the gate.

## What this plan deliberately avoids

- A second action currency, population simulation, or upkeep bookkeeping in the stead
  layer (research/12).
- Area before density: no new region opens thinner than the valley (research/11, 13).
- Answering repetition with template count instead of consequence (research/11).
- Generator changes inside a live world; they land at world boundaries only
  (research/13).
- Silent scale caps or decorative events that fail the fanfic test (research/13).
