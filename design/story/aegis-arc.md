# The Aegis Arc (trans-world main story)

**Status: ADOPTED (D-026).** Canon A "The Ledger" was chosen 2026-07-16; this document is the arc's spec. Rejected alternative canons are preserved in section 10 for the record. Names are canon per D-043 (section 11).

Grounding: D-010, D-013, D-019, D-020 in `../decisions.md`; technique source is the FFXIV study at `../../research/11-ffxiv-storytelling/00-synthesis.md`. Everything here must be deliverable as text storylets, topic entries, and line banks (no cutscenes, no voice, no runtime LLM).

---

## 1. Inherited constraints

- The Aegis is an ancient bound intelligence fastened to the player: it catches the soul at death, anchors rest points, carries the bearer across worlds at NG+, and speaks at deaths, discoveries, and crossings (D-010, D-019).
- The arc advances only at world completions and NG+ crossings, unfolds over the first several cycles, and resolves into an earned steady state (D-020).
- Worlds are always freshly generated; deeper NG+ tiers are harder by generation, not multiplier (D-011).
- Past player characters enter world mythology (D-013).
- Guest companions can permanently die; the player cannot (D-024).
- Attribute respec is a rare, lore-flavored "unbinding" of the Aegis (D-016).
- Essence: the Aegis gathers the essence of deeds and reshapes the bearer at rest points (D-014).

## 2. Technique commitments (from the FFXIV study)

1. **Withhold significance, not facts.** The arc's biggest reveals re-file material the player already has (the Aegis's own stock lines, the Essence loop, the respec vendor), rather than introducing new proper nouns.
2. **Every touch completes a micro-reveal.** No beat is a pure tease; each arc scene answers something small and true.
3. **Concentrated, not smeared.** Arc content is roughly 15 percent of a world's storylets, delivered as cold open, optional bottle episode (milestone worlds only), closing stinger, and the crossing scene. The crossing scene is the arc's guaranteed real estate.
4. **Delete test.** Any world-story must still stand if every arc scene is stripped out.
5. **Antagonists are a pool, plus one fixed anchor.** Beatable instances of a class carry menace across worlds; exactly one hand-authored constant character is allowed the "hidden identity" trick.
6. **No beat requires the player to continue.** Every crossing lands as a complete, satisfying beat even if the player never starts another world.
7. **The mystery's resolution is never the final emotional note.** Personal and local material closes last.

## 3. Canon A: The Ledger (adopted)

Spoiler order note: this section states the full truth up front for the designer. Players receive it via the ladder in section 6.

**The Chain.** The worlds are real, plural, and ordered: links on a chain descending toward the place where worlds are kindled, called the **Hearth**. Worlds nearer the Hearth are older, stranger, and hungrier: this is the diegetic reading of World Hostility Tiers. Each crossing carries the bearer one link deeper.

**The Aegis.** A made thing: a ward-intelligence forged in the last age of a dying order, the **Shieldwrights**, and sent out along the Chain with a mandate: *find and temper a soul fit to keep the Hearth*. It binds to one bearer at a time. It catches the soul at death because a broken bearer is a failed commission. It gathers the essence of deeds because deeds are the measure it was built to take: the Aegis is, among other things, a scale and a ledger. Crucially, **the Aegis does not fully remember any of this at the start.** Long service has worn it; its memory returns in pieces as it refills across cycles. The mystery drip is therefore honest: the Aegis is not coy, it is recovering. It learns the truth of itself only a step ahead of the player, and some steps behind.

**The Severed.** Former bearers. Some cut themselves loose; some had wards that broke and abandoned them mid-Chain. A severed bearer does not die and does not renew: they linger in whatever world they were lost in, unaging and slowly unraveling. Some are sane, courteous, and terribly sad. Some are monstrous. They recognize what the player carries on sight. They are the arc's recurring physical antagonist class: individually castable, individually beatable, collectively inexhaustible, and every one of them is a mirror ("this is you, if it goes wrong").

**The Unbinder.** The fixed anchor. The first bearer: the one who reached the threshold, refused the keeping, and cut themself away before the Aegis (younger then) could stop them. They have walked the Chain far longer than anyone and appear in every world in a different guise but the same true self. They are the game's respec service: loosening the Aegis's grip on a bearer's shape is simply what they know how to do. Their position, never disproved, only countered: *endings are what make anything mean anything. The Chain is ending. Let it. No soul should be carried past its own death to feed an old fire.* They never fight the player. They are unfailingly polite. They may be right.

**The purpose.** The Hearth is where worlds are lit, and its keeping has been empty since the Shieldwrights' age ended. An unkept Hearth still burns, but wrongly: worlds kindle wilder and crueler the deeper the Chain goes (hostility tiers again), and the Severed accumulate like slag. The Aegis's commission is to deliver a soul with enough weighed, lived deed-weight to take up the keeping. Every prior bearer broke, refused, or was lost before the threshold. The player is not the chosen one; the player is the current attempt.

**The truth about the mythology.** The Chain's worlds kindle partly from what crossing souls carry. Statues, ballads, tombs of past player characters (D-013) are diegetically real: echoes of every bearer this Aegis has carried, and of the player's other characters, pressed into new worlds at their kindling. The player's multiple characters are, in fiction, fellow bearers along the same Chain.

## 4. The motif and the voice

**The motif: "All is counted."** The Aegis's stock consolation, spoken from the first death onward. It is planted everywhere a fixed anchor can be planted: shrine inscriptions in a dead script, Shieldwright ruins, the mouths of the Severed (distorted: "All is counted, little shield"), the Unbinder's farewell (gentle, pitying: "Nothing needs to be counted"). Its meaning flips twice:

- Early register: comfort. Your deeds are not wasted; nothing you do is lost.
- Mid register (after the ledger reveal): dread. You are being *tallied*. Toward what?
- Final register (after resolution): chosen. The count is yours now; you decide what is worth weighing.

**Death narration registers.** The Aegis speaks at every death (Hades pattern). Line banks are keyed to arc stage, so the same mechanical moment reads differently as the relationship deepens: terse and functional in cycle 1, personal and worried by cycle 3, candid between equals after resolution. Death lines are the arc's cheapest, highest-frequency delivery surface; they carry register, never plot.

## 5. Arc content budget per world

- **Cold open:** one scene at world arrival (the crossing's landing). Always present.
- **Ambient anchors:** motif inscriptions, bearer-myth topic entries, Severed rumors. Fact-graph seeded, zero scene cost.
- **Bottle episode:** a quarantined, arc-only storylet cluster. Milestone worlds only (cycles 3 and 5 in the ladder below). This is where concentrated wordcount is allowed.
- **Closing stinger:** one scene, post-climax of the world-story, seeding the next rung.
- **Crossing scene:** the between-worlds scene at NG+. The arc's richest guaranteed real estate; every rung of the ladder lands its main micro-reveal here.

Total: roughly one arc storylet per five to six world-story storylets, weighted to the ends of a world, never spread through the middle acts.

## 6. The reveal ladder (cycle by cycle)

Each rung is a complete micro-reveal. "Crossing N" means the scene between world N and world N+1.

**Cycle 1: the hand you did not see.**
- Cold open: first awakening; the voice introduces itself only as what it does ("I catch you. Rest here; I hold this place"). Motif planted at first death.
- Ambient: one Severed appears once, unlabeled: a stranger who looks at the player too long, says the motif wrong, and leaves. No fight, no explanation (significance withheld).
- Crossing 1 micro-reveal: **there are other worlds, and this has happened before.** "I have carried others. I do not remember how many. That frightens me, and I do not remember how to be frightened."

*Implementation status (D-037): cycle 1's ambient Severed, all of cycle 2, and cycle 3's vision + Unbinder tier 1 + ledger crossing are live as flag-gated content (rungs gate on Player arc flags, not cycle counts). The cycle-2 Severed is arc-cast into its own site (the hollow, tier 2+, recurring) rather than template-cast, preserving the delete test. The cycle-3 bottle landed as a witnessed text vision at the shrine. Crossing scenes are AegisVoice line banks keyed to pending flags.*

**Cycle 2: the failures.**
- Cold open: the Aegis has recovered a fragment: its maker's word for itself (a forge-name), which it turns over like a coin all cycle.
- Ambient: bearer-myths begin surfacing in topic systems: tombs of "those who did not die", ballads that are recognizably about past player characters where they exist, authored prior bearers where they do not.
- Designed encounter: the world-story casts one **Severed** as a mid-arc threat. Beating them earns the post-fight reveal: **the stranger-kind are former bearers.** They call the player "the new grip on the old shield."
- Crossing 2 micro-reveal: the Aegis confirms it: **the Severed are its own lost bearers**, and it names the two ways they are made (cut loose, or dropped when a ward breaks). First admission of guilt.

**Cycle 3 (milestone): the forging. Bottle episode.**
- Bottle: a recovered-memory vision cluster (the Amaurot slot): the Shieldwrights' last age, the forging of the wards, the commission spoken over the anvil. The memory cuts off before the commission's *object*: the Aegis reaches the edge of what it has re-remembered and stops. Micro-reveal: **what the Aegis is (a made thing under orders). Not yet what the orders are.**
- The Unbinder, whom the player has almost certainly already used for respec, is confronted knowingly for the first time. Reveal tier 1: they are older than this world, and the Aegis hates them with an intimacy it cannot explain. The Unbinder declines to elaborate: "Ask it what it counts."
- Crossing 3 micro-reveal: **the ledger.** Essence is not only for the bearer: a tithe of every deed's weight has been going somewhere else all along. The player's own grind is recontextualized in one line. The Aegis did not lie; it did not remember. It is not certain which is worse.

**Cycle 4: the argument.**
- Two sympathetic Severed storylets, structurally different by design: one **agency-model** (chose to cut loose, fully self-aware, at peace and quietly persuasive: the Unbinder's argument wearing a face the player might like) and one **essence-model** (ward-dropped, no choice, unraveling, held together by routines they no longer understand: the cost wearing a face the player must pity). Neither fight is mandatory; both can end without violence.
- The Unbinder, reveal tier 2 at a gated confrontation (requires both Severed storylets or equivalent fact-graph weight): **they are the first bearer**, and the refusal at the threshold is told from their side, as the proudest thing they ever did. Their offer is stated plainly for later: when the player reaches the threshold, the Unbinder will be there, and will offer to cut them free before the choice is forced.
- Crossing 4 micro-reveal: the Aegis remembers the rest. **The Hearth, the empty keeping, the commission in full.** It says the words it was forged around, and then, unprompted, the thing no one built it to say: "You may refuse. I will carry you either way."

*Implementation status (D-038): all of cycle 4 is live, gated on the ledger flag. The agency-model Severed is a recurring tier-3+ NPC (hermit at a fire, talk-only: the engine has no verb for attacking a person, so the no-violence guarantee is structural); the essence-model beat reuses the hollow's keeper, witnessed from the threshold stones via an EnterTile storylet before any choice to enter, so the recurring fight the player already knows is re-filed rather than a new character introduced. Reveal tier 2 requires both witnesses on top of tier 1 and unlocks "The refusal" topic; the commission is a crossing line bank finishing the vision's cut-off sentence. A fight-or-spare choice for Severed met as people is deferred until an attack-NPC verb exists.*

**Cycle 5 (milestone): the threshold. Bottle episode and arc climax.**
- The world generates normally, but its deepest authored site is the arc's fixed final stage: a Hearth-approach (one bottle site inside a procedural world, not a fixed world).
- The threshold scene: the Hearth, the empty keeping, the Unbinder present as promised, the Aegis speaking as a party to the choice rather than a narrator of it. The choice (section 8): **take up the keeping on your own terms, or refuse and walk on unbound in spirit.** Both resolve the mystery; neither ends the game.
- Mandated final beat: NOT the mystery. A witnessed, low-mechanics epilogue with this world's companions and consequences: the arc closes on the local and personal (technique commitment 7).

*Implementation status (D-039): all of cycle 5 is live, gated on the commission flag. The Hearth-approach is an authored bottle site generated in every tier-5+ world (the site by tier, since worldgen cannot read character state; the door by flag, waygate-pattern, so the ladder's timing tolerance holds). The approach is pure walk and text, no gatekeeper fight (sec 11's dressing question, resolved: cycle 4 made no-violence structural and the finale honors it); the Unbinder keeps the rung-4 promise at the door; the choice is a journaled two-entry menu at the Hearth that waits indefinitely and closes forever once answered. Both endings set one flag; a reflection test plus a live two-session pilot diff enforce the sec 8 guardrail field by field. The mandated final beat is a witnessed stead epilogue storylet, Aegis silent, closing line local. Register swaps landed with it: death lines in three registers (terse, worried after the ledger, candid after resolution), per-answer crossing lines, the rest line, and a per-answer persistent Unbinder topic carrying the unfinished argument.*

**Cycle 6 onward: steady state (section 9).**

Timing tolerance: "cycle N" above means "Nth world completion" on the default path, but every rung's storylets are precondition-gated on the previous rung, not on a hard cycle count, so slow players who linger and fast players who beeline hit the same ladder in the same order.

## 7. The antagonist kit

**The Severed (pool).**
- Cast per world from a role-slot template family, like any storylet cast, but drawing on a small authored trait deck (era, manner of severing, what they kept, what they lost) so instances feel individually authored.
- Two sympathy models, never merged: agency-model and essence-model (section 6, cycle 4). Instances outside those two designed storylets may be plain threats; not every Severed is sympathetic.
- Defeating a Severed never deflates the class (pool principle): each defeat pays out one card of truth, never the deck.
- Post-resolution, the pool inverts from menace to responsibility (section 9).

**The Unbinder (fixed anchor).**
- The single permitted use of the hidden-identity trick, because they are the single hand-authored constant besides the Aegis itself.
- Layered identity by trust and escalation, never by clock: a guise-name in each world, then "older than this world" (cycle 3), then "the first bearer" (cycle 4), each behind fact-graph gates.
- Their worldview is never disproved. Their topic-system entries stay coherent and unyielding no matter how often or late they are raised. Post-resolution they remain in the world, still courteous, still unbinding, unconverted.
- They never fight the player. The game never makes it possible.
- Standing irony, surfaced but never resolved: the mercy they offer (unbinding) is the same act that makes essence-model Severed possible. They know. It costs them.

**The mirror discipline.** Every Severed encounter and every Unbinder scene is written to put the same question in a different mouth: *what is a life that cannot end worth?* The world-stories answer with lived material (mortal companions, D-024, carry the stakes the player cannot); the arc answers at the threshold.

## 8. The threshold choice and its guardrail

Two resolutions, both valid, both resolving the mystery into a changed relationship:

- **Keep the Hearth (on your terms).** Not retirement into a room with a fire: the keeping *is* the crossing. A kept Hearth kindles worlds that are dangerous but not cruel for cruelty's sake, and the keeper feeds it exactly the way the player already plays: completed worlds, weighed deeds, seeded legends. The loop the player was going to do anyway becomes, in fiction, the most important job that exists.
- **Refuse, and walk on.** The Unbinder's path, taken with the Aegis rather than against it: the commission is laid down, the tithe ends, and bearer and ward continue as companions by choice. The Hearth stays unkept; the deep worlds stay wild; the player owns that.

**Guardrail: the endings differ in fiction, voice register, and steady-state flavor text. They never differ in mechanics.** Same NG+ loop, same covenants, same death rules, same content access. Aegis cannot afford two mechanically divergent endgames, and the design must not punish either answer to its central question.

Either way, the diegetic frame for **covenants** upgrades at resolution: the terms of each crossing become the bearer's to set. Choosing covenants at world creation is, from here on, the player character doing it in fiction.

## 9. Steady state (post-resolution cycles)

- The Aegis's voice changes register permanently: candid, occasionally funny, an equal. Death lines, crossing lines, and banter banks all swap to the final register.
- Arc-adjacent content continues as small, self-contained storylet families, each a complete short story, roughly 10 to 15 percent of a world's content, in the spirit of good post-saga patch cycles: new Severed encountered (the player can now fight, unbind, or in rare authored cases restore them), Unbinder visits, Hearth-signs in deep worlds, bearer-myths of the player's own past characters compounding.
- **No new grand mystery is layered on top of the resolved one.** The Chain, the Hall of Legends, covenants, and fresh world-stories carry motivation from here (D-020). The one permitted long thread: the Aegis and the Unbinder's unfinished argument, advanced a line at a time, never re-mystified.

*Implementation status (D-045): steady state v1 is live. The laying-down verb (fight or unbind the hollow's keeper post-resolution, via a journaled menu, same verb and price for both answers per the sec 8 guardrail); the Unbinder argument thread (sequenced beats, at most one per cycle, per-answer opening, unhurried close at stage three), preempted once by the trade-shared beat when the bearer first unbinds a Severed (the sec 7 standing irony, surfaced); hearth-signs in tier-4+ worlds (per answer, text only, once per world); the compounding long song (the character carries the walked worlds, a song fact compounds from the third crossing, and a once-ever stead beat sings the descent back wrong while the final register corrects it); and the hermit's per-answer reaction. Restoring a Severed remains the deferred rare authored case.*

## 10. Alternative canons (rejected, preserved for the record)

- **Canon B: The Loom.** The worlds are woven: dreams or workings of a Weaver at the Chain's root, and the Aegis is a shuttle carrying true souls through the weave. Boldest metaphysics (procgen itself becomes diegetic), and the mythology mechanic reads beautifully (the Loom reuses threads). Risk: resolves toward "the worlds are not real," which can retroactively cheapen every mortal companion the player buried, and the design leans hard on those stakes.
- **Canon C: The Warden.** Inversion: the Aegis is a seal, and the bearer is the vessel for something sealed. Resurrection is containment; worlds harden because the seal wears. Strongest single twist in the set and the darkest tone. Risk: it makes the player's power the world's problem, which fights the fantasy of earned heroism (D-005's humble starts are aspirational, not guilty), and the twist spends once where Canon A's ledger re-files continuously.
- **Why Canon A is recommended:** it is the only canon where every existing mechanic becomes load-bearing story with no retrofit: Essence (the scale), respec (the antagonist's service), covenants (earned terms), hostility tiers (depth on the Chain), mythology (bearer echoes), the infinite loop itself (the keeping). Its reveals recontextualize the player's own grind rather than external lore, which is the study's single strongest technique.

## 11. Open questions for a future session

- Final names: Aegis's forge-name, the Shieldwrights, the Chain, the Hearth, the Severed, the Unbinder. *Resolved by D-043: the working names are canon. "Skeld" (AegisVoice.ForgeName, picked in D-037) is final: a maker's plain shield-word for a made shield. The epithets stay as plain weighted English, which is what authored names sound like in this game's register; coined names are reserved for persons and made things (the forge-name, NPC name pools). Two authored name-facts landed in content to make the keeps deliberate: the Shieldwrights' own name for themselves is lost to the Aegis's worn memory (vision storylet), and the Unbinder laid their name down with everything else (reveal-tier-2 topic), the mirror of the Aegis getting its name back.*
- The Unbinder's guise design per world: how strongly disguised, and what constant tells (a phrase, a service, a mannerism) let attentive players clock them early. Early-clocking should be possible and rewarded (the study's hindsight-reread principle).
- Whether the cycle-3 bottle vision is playable (the player briefly acts in the memory) or witnessed (text vision). Playable is stronger and costlier. *D-037 shipped witnessed for v1; upgrading to playable remains open.*
- The exact mechanical dressing of the threshold scene (pure dialogue, or a final trial fight against a designed Severed "gatekeeper"). *D-039 resolved this: pure approach and dialogue, no gatekeeper fight, because cycle 4 made no-violence structural for Severed met as people and the finale must not unsay it.*
- Whether multiple player characters share Unbinder reveal-tier progress (world mythology already crosses characters via D-013; reveal tiers probably should not).
