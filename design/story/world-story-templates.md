# World-Story Spine Templates

**Status: DRAFT.** Structure and the three launch templates per D-020. Canon-light: nothing here depends on which Aegis-arc canon is chosen; arc attachment points are marked generically.

Grounding: D-018 (narrative engine), D-020 (two-layer main quest), D-023 (factions), D-024 (companions). Technique source: `../../research/11-ffxiv-storytelling/00-synthesis.md`. A world-story is the authored main-quest spine of one generated world: structure and beats handwritten, cast and geography filled from that world's fact graph. Beeline 10-15 hours; the world holds 30+.

---

## 1. The template contract

Every template must define:

1. **Logline** and the dramatic question it poses.
2. **Worldgen preconditions**: which fact-graph facts must exist for the template to be eligible (the generator selects among eligible templates per world; every template needs modest preconditions so eligibility is common).
3. **The accepted-history fact**: one piece of "everyone knows" backstory planted early as ambient topic flavor, never a flagged quest, which the mid-turn flips with evidence the player finds.
4. **Role slots** with casting preconditions and fallbacks (cast from existing world NPCs, Wildermyth pattern).
5. **Act beats** with rough storylet budgets and the facts each beat writes and reads.
6. **Two to three endings**, differing in world-state consequences (never in mechanical reward tier).
7. **Coda writes**: what the finished story leaves in the fact graph for patronage (D-025), mythology (D-013), and the closing stinger.
8. **Arc attachment points**: where the cold open, optional bottle episode, and closing stinger dock (and nothing else; see iron rule 2).

## 2. Iron rules (apply to every template)

1. **The delete test.** Strip every Aegis-arc scene out; the remainder must still be a complete three-act story with its own setup, villain, and resolution. A template whose climax needs meta-arc knowledge is miswritten.
2. **Arc budget.** Roughly 15 percent of the world's storylets are arc content, concentrated at open, optional mid bottle, close, and the crossing. The escalation acts are arc-free.
3. **Thread discipline.** Never more than two or three active storylet threads at once, and **every subplot storylet writes at least one fact that a later storylet, ideally the climax, reads.** A thread whose facts are never read again gets cut or folded into flavor. This is the anti-ARR-slog rule and the most cited failure in the study.
4. **The convergence point is singular.** Local plot and any arc tag are allowed to collide exactly once, at the climax.
5. **Witnessed ending.** Immediately after the climax: a short, low-mechanics, dialogue-only scene with one or two speaking characters and two to four silent role-cast witnesses. The quiet scene, not the fight, is where the payoff lands; it is mandatory in every template and every ending.
6. **Coda: promotion and retirement, not vanishing.** Surviving story companions exit to a **portfolio** (a standing off-screen role the fact graph can reference in flavor) or a **retirement storylet** (dignified in-character exit). A companion death always writes a **promotion fact** naming who inherits their narrative weight: death is redistribution, never subtraction.
7. **Rival spacing.** The physical antagonist is met two to three times, spaced by enforced elapsed world-time preconditions, and the final payoff is verbal or relational, not a fourth fight.
8. **Banked-death gating.** A companion death storylet may only fire once a minimum count of small banked loyalty beats exists in the fact graph, and is always followed by a mandatory, mechanically inert epilogue scene.
9. **Beat-type tracking.** The engine records fired beat-types per NPC (betrayal, near-death, possession-equivalent, redemption) and never repeats one on the same character.
10. **Gather-N variety.** Any collect-from-N-parties structure must draw each sub-quest from a different storylet archetype pool, enforced by the template, or procgen will surface the repetition brutally.
11. **Ensemble cap.** Emotional scenes: one to two speakers, at most two to four silent witnesses. No full-ensemble reaction scenes; the game has no fixed decade-old cast to make them land.
12. **No lore-only storylets.** Every reveal storylet also has a concrete mechanical postcondition (a path opens, an NPC becomes available, a fact enables a later quest).

## 3. Shared act skeleton

1. **Hook** (2-4 storylets): the local problem, on the world's own terms. Optional low-weight arc cold open docks before this, never inside it.
2. **Local setup** (4-6): antagonists introduced, companions role-cast, accepted-history fact planted as ambient topic flavor.
3. **Escalation** (the bulk): two to three threads under iron rule 3. Rival encounter 1 lands here.
4. **Mid-turn** (1-3): the accepted-history flip, via evidence the player actively finds. Consequences cascade from the fact graph (faction shifts, price changes, dialogue swaps), not hand-script.
5. **Deepening** (second escalation block): threads converge pairwise; rival encounter 2; companion arcs peak (banked-death window opens here at the earliest).
6. **Climax** (2-4): the single convergence point. Assembled allies are cast dynamically by querying who the player materially helped, never hand-permuted.
7. **Witnessed ending** (1): iron rule 5.
8. **Coda** (1-2): promotions, retirements, patronage window, world-state settling.
9. **Closing stinger** (1): arc scene, gated on the world having written enough of its own facts.

## 4. Shared role-slot library

Slots any template may cast; each maps to techniques the study validated. Casting prefers NPCs the fact graph already made relevant (existing relationships beat invented strangers).

| Slot | Function | Key discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Banked-ordinary companion | The heart; their (optional) death cashes dozens of small logged beats | Iron rules 8, 6 |
| Measuring-stick rival | The physical antagonist; 2-3 spaced encounters, one consistent motive revealed incrementally | Iron rule 7 |
| Complicit authority | Trusted figure whose betrayal triggers on a player action (an item delivered or recovered), never on exposition | Beat-type tracked |
| Debunked-but-transcendent believer | Faith established early, mechanism disproved mid-arc, value affirmed late; the debunking must sting | Core of Template 3 |
| Redeemable antagonist | Backstory via discoverable object or vision; flippable at a precondition-gated moment | Two sympathy models across a cast, never one |
| Portfolio-holder | Companion who exits into a standing job, referenceable in flavor | Iron rule 6 |
| Silent witness / memorial proxy | Keeps a dead companion alive through anecdote in later storylets | Makes deaths keep paying |
| One-note fixed-axis rival | Single unchanging throughline; instantly legible under procedural re-casting | Cheap, reusable |

## 5. Template 1: The Usurped Throne

**Logline.** The power that rules this land was taken, not given; the taking was lied about; and the lie is now load-bearing. Dramatic question: what do you owe the truth when the lie is holding the roof up?

**Worldgen preconditions.** A dominant polity with a personal ruler (crown, high seat, archon); a succession event in generated history within living memory; at least one surviving displaced line or faction. (The generator's causal history makes this common; "throne" reads broadly: a guild-throne or temple-seat qualifies.)

**Accepted-history fact.** The official account of how the current ruler came to power (the old line died of plague; abdicated; fell in a just war). Planted in topic entries, tavern talk, an anniversary festival.

**Roles.** Ruler-by-lie (redeemable antagonist, agency model: knows exactly what they did, believes it was right, and the land has objectively prospered); the Claimant (debunked-but-transcendent believer: their claim's *evidence* may crumble mid-arc even as their fitness grows); the ruler's champion (measuring-stick rival, loyal for reasons the player comes to respect); the chancellor or hierophant who consecrated the lie (complicit authority); a retainer of the old line (banked-ordinary companion).

**Threads.** (a) Backing: win factions to the Claimant, each via a different archetype pool (iron rule 10); (b) Evidence: unearth what actually happened, feeding the mid-turn; (c) Rival: the champion thread.

**Mid-turn.** The evidence flips the accepted history, and lands one complication: the old line was not innocent either, or the usurpation prevented something worse. The flip must complicate, not just invert, or the ending choice is fake.

**Climax.** Confrontation at the seat with dynamically cast backers present. Endings: restore the Claimant (stability facts drop, justice facts rise); confirm the ruler with the truth published (legitimacy rebuilt on confession); broker a third settlement (council, marriage of lines, partition) if specific mid-game facts were written.

**Coda writes.** Who rules and on what story; the champion's fate (portfolio: marshal, exile, or grave plus memorial proxy); a "the-truth-of-the-taking" fact that future worlds' mythology can echo.

**Arc attachment.** Stinger docks on the new ruler's first decree; bottle episode, when this world is a milestone, uses the dynasty's crypts (old-line tombs suit recovered-memory content).

## 6. Template 2: The Creeping Blight

**Logline.** Something is wrong with the land itself, it is spreading, and the story everyone tells about it is wrong. Dramatic question: what does the comfortable center owe the eaten edge?

**Worldgen preconditions.** A frontier or hinterland region flagged blightable (any biome; the blight reskins: rot, ash-creep, silence, salt); a prosperous core region economically upstream of it; at least one institution chartered to deal with such things (an order, a college, a church).

**Accepted-history fact.** What the blight is and whose fault: a curse from the old war, the anger of a god, the filth of the blighted people themselves. The institution teaches it; border towns act on it (refugee turn-aways write early facts the climax reads).

**Roles.** The Warden of the source (redeemable antagonist, essence model: bound, no memory or choice, holding the worst of it back even as it consumes them); the institution's zealot field-commander who burns villages ahead of the line (measuring-stick rival: effective, wrong, sincere); the institution's head who knows the true cause (complicit authority); a blight-touched healer or scholar (debunked-but-transcendent believer: their faith's mechanism is disproved, their care is affirmed); a border guide (banked-ordinary companion; this template's banked-death window is the most natural in the set, and remains optional).

**Threads.** (a) The line: triage and defense along the blight-front, each stop a different archetype pool; (b) The cause: fieldwork and archive work toward the mid-turn; (c) Rival: the zealot thread.

**Mid-turn.** The blight is downstream of the core's prosperity: a paid-for working, a broken old bargain, a mine into something that should have stayed shut. The comfortable center caused the eaten edge and its institution has always known.

**Climax.** At the source, with the Warden. Endings: end the blight by paying its true cost back onto the core (economy facts shift hard); contain it and publish the truth (ongoing management, political fallout); free or restore the Warden if the empathy gates were met (rarest, requires specific mid-game finds).

**Coda writes.** The blight-front's new border; the institution reformed, shattered, or doubled down; the zealot's fate; regional price and scarcity facts (D-025 hooks are strongest here).

**Arc attachment.** This template's material (consumption, essence, a bound guardian) resonates with the arc's themes; the stinger dock is written to exploit that resonance without depending on it (delete test holds). Bottle episode site: the source itself, after the climax settles.

## 7. Template 3: The War of Faiths

**Logline.** Two faiths are at war over an origin both misremember. Dramatic question: when belief and truth part ways, which do people actually need?

**Worldgen preconditions.** Two organized faiths with adjacent or interleaved territory; a shared sacred site, relic, or founder-figure in the generated history; an active or freshly ignited conflict between them. One side is designated the current aggressor at casting time: the war must have a present-tense wrong even though the origin is shared. Both-sides mush is this template's death.

**Accepted-history fact.** Each faith's account of the schism (the other side's founder was a betrayer, a thief of revelation, a false prophet). Two versions of the same planted fact, told against each other in topic entries on each side of the border.

**Roles.** A believer-champion on each side (the aggressor's is the measuring-stick rival; the defender's is the debunked-but-transcendent believer and the template's heart); the keeper of the founding site who knows what the founding actually was (complicit authority by silence rather than malice); a war-orphan or convert straddling both faiths (banked-ordinary companion); optionally a war-profiteer feeding both sides (one-note fixed-axis rival, comic-dark register).

**Threads.** (a) The front: stop or survive the war's escalations; (b) The founding: pilgrimage and excavation toward the truth; (c) The champions: parallel relationship arcs with both believer-champions (this template's rival encounters are split across two characters by design).

**Mid-turn.** The founding truth: one miracle, shared, then split by the founders' human quarrel (property, jealousy, grief); or a fabrication both inherited innocently. The debunking must sting: the defender-champion's faith takes the hit on screen, and the template's late storylets affirm the *value* their faith produced (the hospices, the ransomed captives) without un-disproving the mechanism.

**Climax.** At the shared site, both champions present, truth in hand. Endings: publish the truth and broker a shared custody (both institutions wounded, peace facts written); suppress the truth to end the war on a usable lie (peace now, a new accepted-history fact planted for THIS world's future, the game's most explicit moral trade); side with one faith's reformation and let it absorb the other (strongest faction realignment).

**Coda writes.** The site's custody; both faiths' reformation state; the champions' fates (the surviving pair as portfolio-holders on opposite sides is the strong default); a founding-truth fact with unusually high mythology weight (wars of faith echo loudly in future worlds).

**Arc attachment.** Stinger docks at the site the night after the settlement. The believer material gives the arc's own question (what is owed to a guiding power that is not what it claims) its sharpest local echo; as always, the echo is flavor, not dependency.

## 8. Casting, eligibility, and variation

- **Eligibility filtering.** The generator scores each template against the world's fact graph and picks among eligible templates (weighted against recent repeats across the player's cycles). Every template's preconditions are deliberately modest; a world that fails all three should be effectively impossible at launch counts. *Landed as v1 in D-035: templates declare eligibility over a worldgen context, one is drawn per world from the world-story stream, and the Creeping Blight compiles at slice scale against the barrow (accepted-history flip and branching endings both proven). Repeat-weighting landed in D-040: the crossing passes the finished world's template id into the next draw as a generation input, and an eligible repeat carries half the weight of every other candidate (weighted, never excluded, so back-to-back repeats stay possible and strict alternation never sets in).*
- **Fallback casting.** Every role slot defines a fallback chain (e.g. Claimant: surviving heir, then cadet-line cousin, then chartered pretender-institution). If a slot's whole chain fails, the template is ineligible; slots are never filled by inventing an unrooted stranger (D-018's traceability rule).
- **Variation axes.** Per template, the axes procgen legitimately varies: scale (village to empire), institution skin (crown, guild, temple), tone tilt (tragic, hopeful, sardonic, set by the world's history), geography binding, and which optional roles cast. The spine's beats never vary; that is what "authored structure" means.
- **Repeat encounters across cycles.** A player will eventually replay a template. Defenses: variation axes above, the accepted-history fact's *content* always comes from this world's unique history, endings branch on this world's facts, and the pool grows post-launch (D-020: launch 3, growing).
