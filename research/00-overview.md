# Aegis: Research Overview and Synthesis

## Game vision

Aegis is a terminal based (TUI), single player, turn based RPG that aims for the tone and depth of Elden Ring and Kingdom Come: Deliverance without real time action. There are no character classes: stats, skills, and equipment alone determine what a character can do and how well. The world is mostly procedurally generated, but it carries real storylines, real NPCs, real quests, and real dialogue rather than the "slot machine" feel typical of procedural content. Death is a meaningful setback, never a permanent end. No two playthroughs are the same, and the game supports infinite New Game Plus (NG+, NG++, and so on): finishing the game lets a player carry a character into a freshly generated, harder world. Players can maintain multiple saved characters, each with a chosen starting race, starting stats, and starting gear.

## Index of research files

1. **[01-procedural-world-plus-authored-narrative.md](01-procedural-world-plus-authored-narrative.md)**. Surveys how Dwarf Fortress, Caves of Qud, Wildermyth, Shadow of Mordor/War, Failbetter's storylet games, Skyrim, and Kenshi each reconcile procedural generation with narrative that feels authored. The consistent answer is a fixed authored "spine" (anchors, history, main quest) combined with a storylet or memory driven system that fills in variation, since visible templates ("mad libs") are the single biggest failure mode across every game studied.

2. **[02-classless-progression-systems.md](02-classless-progression-systems.md)**. Compares use-based, point-buy, and equipment-gated progression across Kingdom Come: Deliverance, the Elder Scrolls series, Dark Souls/Elden Ring, Caves of Qud, Cataclysm: Dark Days Ahead, and Path of Exile. The recurring lesson is that use-based skills need hard, non-trivial costs to resist grinding exploits, and that soft-capped stats plus visible gear requirements (not a class label) are what make a classless build feel legible and distinct.

3. **[03-death-as-setback-not-permadeath.md](03-death-as-setback-not-permadeath.md)**. Reviews death penalty design in Dark Souls/Elden Ring, Hades, Rogue Legacy, Wildermyth, Kenshi, Darkest Dungeon, and the MMO death penalty arc from EverQuest through World of Warcraft. The industry has converged on splitting losses into recoverable, run scoped resources versus permanently banked character progress, keeping penalties diegetic and time or currency based rather than power reducing, and reserving true permanence (scars, reputation) for rare, legible triggers.

4. **[04-infinite-new-game-plus.md](04-infinite-new-game-plus.md)**. Examines NG+ and endless scaling systems in Elden Ring, Hades, Slay the Spire, Dead Cells, Diablo, Path of Exile, Rogue Legacy, Grim Dawn, Risk of Rain 2, and incremental game "prestige" design. Flat numeric scaling caps out and telegraphs its own irrelevance (Elden Ring stops mattering past NG+7), while modular, player composed difficulty and discrete content tiers (Hades' Heat, Dead Cells' Boss Stem Cells) keep escalation meaningful without an unbounded formula that outruns its own content, as Risk of Rain 2's late game emptiness demonstrates.

5. **[05-terminal-ui-tech-stack.md](05-terminal-ui-tech-stack.md)**. Compares TUI frameworks (Terminal.Gui, ratatui, Textual, Ink, and others) across rendering, input, shipping, and testability, then covers save game architecture and deterministic RNG for procedural worlds. Rust with ratatui and crossterm is the leading recommendation for the game engine itself, paired with a hybrid seed-plus-delta save format and a hierarchical, hashed seed tree so world regeneration and NG+ chaining stay deterministic and small on disk.

6. **[06-quest-and-dialogue-generation.md](06-quest-and-dialogue-generation.md)**. Surveys academic quest pattern taxonomies, Failbetter's storylet ("quality based narrative") model, Wildermyth's role-casting scenes, Caves of Qud's history driven villages, the Nemesis system's memory driven dialogue, and RimWorld's pacing director. The unifying fix for the "radiant quest" problem is to generate a fact and relationship graph first and treat quests and dialogue as queries or templates over that graph, never as free invention disconnected from world state.

## Cross-cutting design implications

The six research areas are not independent. Several concrete interactions surfaced across them and should shape the architecture from the start, not be retrofitted later.

- **One world state graph feeds narrative, death consequences, and quest generation simultaneously.** Files 01, 03, and 06 independently converge on the same mechanism: a persistent memory or fact record attached to specific NPCs, factions, and locations (the Nemesis pattern). That single system should back the storylet precondition/effect variables (01, 06), the NPC "who remembers your death" hooks (03), and the fact graph that quest generation queries (06). Building three separate systems for these would duplicate the hardest infrastructure in the whole project.

- **NG+ world regeneration is the load-bearing constraint on the save system, and the save system is the load-bearing constraint on quest generation.** File 04 requires a genuinely fresh world (not a reset flag) at every NG+ transition. File 05's hybrid seed-plus-delta save model and hierarchical seed tree are what make that regeneration deterministic, reproducible, and small on disk. File 01 and 06's "generate history once per world, make it queryable" pattern only works if that history generation step is itself seeded and versioned the same way, so the quest/dialogue system's fact graph must be built as part of the same seeded world-generation pipeline described in file 05, not as a separate content layer bolted on afterward.

- **Classless progression and death penalties must be designed together, or death becomes either trivial or a de facto class reset.** Because Aegis has no class safety net (file 02), a death penalty that reduces core stats or skills (file 03's cautionary EverQuest example) would be far more crippling than in a class based game, since there is no alternate build path to fall back on. This argues strongly for file 03's recommendation to keep penalties time and currency based rather than power reducing, and to make scars rare, reversible, and narratively flagged rather than a routine tax on every death.

- **Respec economy and NG+ transition should be designed as one lever, not two.** File 02 recommends a rare, costly respec resource to keep early build choices weighty while still allowing course correction. File 04 recommends NG+ transitions as the natural point for consequential, semi-permanent changes. The clean synthesis is to refresh or grant respec resources at NG+ entry, since a harder, unfamiliar regenerated world is exactly when a player most needs the ability to correct a build mistake, and doing so avoids adding a second, unrelated respec economy.

- **NG+ difficulty scaling should follow the same "discrete tier plus new content" shape that file 06 recommends for quest pacing.** File 04's core recommendation (World Hostility Tiers that unlock new enemy types and mechanics, not flat multipliers) pairs directly with file 06's RimWorld-style pacing director: the same tier value that decides how hostile the generated world is can also drive the pacing director's event severity and frequency thresholds, giving one shared difficulty knob instead of two independently tuned ones.

- **Anti-save-scumming architecture and the hybrid save model must be implemented as one write path.** File 03 requires that death consequences (reputation flags, scars, world state changes) be written to disk immediately on death, before the player can reload past them. File 05's hybrid save model already distinguishes "authored/mutated state" (always persisted) from "regenerable state" (seed derived). Death consequences belong entirely in the always-persisted bucket, and the autosave-on-death write described in file 03 is simply a mandatory, immediate flush of that bucket, not a separate mechanism.

- **Equipment-gated build identity (file 02) is itself quest and narrative content (file 06).** Because gear requirements are the primary way a classless game makes stats legible, generated quests and dialogue can and should reference the player's actual stat/skill/equipment state as query conditions (a storylet precondition keyed on "Strength above threshold" or "owns a faction's signature weapon"), turning the progression system into another source of narrative specificity rather than a purely mechanical layer sitting beside the story.

## Key decisions to make

1. **Base narrative architecture.** Leading option: adopt a storylet (quality based narrative) engine as the foundation for every quest, dialogue exchange, and world event, with numeric preconditions and effects over a shared world-state "quality" system (file 01, file 06). This is the only researched pattern that scales additively rather than combinatorially, a hard requirement for content that must keep expanding across infinite NG+ cycles.

2. **History generation timing and scope.** Leading option: generate a causal, queryable history once per world (or NG+ cycle) using replacement grammars over templated cause-effect events, not live simulation, and surface it in-fiction through books, bards, historians, or gravestones (file 01, file 06, following Dwarf Fortress and Caves of Qud).

3. **Progression model shape.** Leading option: use-based skills with hard, non-trivial practice costs (durable tools, consumable ingredients, fatigue, real risk), no attribute-point-per-level abstraction between use and growth, soft caps on every scaling stat, and hard, visible stat/skill requirements on equipment (file 02, following Kingdom Come: Deliverance and Elden Ring).

4. **Respec policy.** Leading option: a rare, discoverable, lore-justified respec resource (an Elden Ring Larval Tear equivalent), refreshed or granted at each NG+ transition rather than offered as unlimited free reallocation or withheld entirely (file 02, file 04).

5. **Death penalty design.** Leading option: split every loss into "run-scoped" (recoverable via a single-chance, genuinely risky corpse run) versus "banked" (stats, skills, owned gear, quest and relationship progress, never lost). Keep the primary penalty a bounded, diegetic mix of time cost, small currency/loot risk, and a temporary debuff, never a stat or skill reduction (file 03, following the industry's retreat from EverQuest-style XP loss).

6. **Permanent consequence triggers.** Leading option: reserve scars, injuries, and reputation stains for a small, tunable minority of deaths (calibrate to roughly one to a handful across an entire campaign, per Wildermyth), gated to specific triggers such as boss-tier deaths or deaths after ignoring clear warning signs, and always paired with a costly but real path back to parity (file 03).

7. **Anti-save-scumming approach.** Leading option: autosave immediately on death, before the penalty is shown, with all consequence state written into that save, while still offering normal manual saves for casual players and an explicit optional Ironman/hardcore toggle for players who want the tension enforced by the save architecture itself (file 03, following the Paradox Ironman and Diablo II Hardcore/Softcore patterns).

8. **NG+ carry-over contract.** Leading option: commit to three explicit buckets: Character (full carry: stats, skills, owned items, known recipes and lore), Meta-progression (a small, diminishing-returns currency or unlock track deliberately weaker than raw character power), and World (always fully regenerated: map, NPCs, factions, quests, dungeons) (file 04, following Elden Ring's stat/equipment carry-over model, extended to a truly new world each cycle).

9. **NG+ difficulty scaling mechanism.** Leading option: scale primarily through discrete "World Hostility Tiers" that unlock new enemy types, hazards, and AI behaviors at each rung, with only a modest, non-compounding stat multiplier attached, optionally layered with Hades-style player-composed modifiers rather than a single global difficulty number (file 04, explicitly avoiding the Elden Ring flat-multiplier cap-out and the Risk of Rain 2 unbacked-scalar failure).

10. **Core engine and language stack.** Leading option: Rust with ratatui and crossterm as the primary TUI stack, chosen for flicker-free rendering, the cleanest self-contained cross-platform binary story, and the strongest first-party testing tools (TestBackend plus insta snapshots) (file 05). C#/.NET with Terminal.Gui v2 is the strongest runner-up if existing team tooling favors it.

11. **Save format and RNG architecture.** Leading option: a hybrid save model, a versioned seed contract for everything regenerable plus real persisted delta state for everything authored or player-mutated, combined with a hierarchical seed tree (SplitMix64-derived child streams per subsystem, region, and roll) so world generation, NG+ chaining, and reproducibility all stay deterministic and small on disk (file 05, following the Caves of Qud and Cataclysm: Dark Days Ahead precedents).

12. **Quest and dialogue generation method.** Leading option: model the world as a fact and relationship graph first, and treat quest and dialogue generation as queries and role-casting over that graph (Wildermyth-style scene roles filled by whichever NPCs currently satisfy them), never as generation from a vacuum; reject or regenerate any content that references an entity not present in world state (file 06).
