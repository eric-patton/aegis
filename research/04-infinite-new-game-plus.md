# Infinite New Game+ Systems: Research for Aegis

## Overview

New Game+ (NG+) is old technology (Chrono Trigger popularized it in 1995) but "infinite NG+ into a freshly generated world" is a much harder problem than the classic "replay the same content with your old stats" loop. Aegis needs three things at once, and most reference games only solve one or two:

1. **A carry-over contract**: what persists across a world regeneration (character, stats, some items/knowledge) versus what resets (the map, NPCs, quests, factions).
2. **A scaling model that stays interesting past the third or fourth cycle**, not just bigger enemy health bars.
3. **A difficulty curve that respects a returning, overpowered character** instead of either trivializing NG+1 or (worse) making it feel identical to a fresh playthrough with a stat tax.

The clearest split in the research is between "same world, harder numbers" games (Souls-likes, Diablo) and "player-curated modular difficulty" games (Hades, Slay the Spire, Dead Cells), plus a third bucket of "infinite math" systems (Greater Rifts, Risk of Rain 2, incremental-game prestige) that show what happens when scaling has no ceiling and no new mechanics to hang it on. Aegis sits at the intersection: it needs Souls-like "your character is real and persists" plus Hades-like "modular, chosen escalation" plus a genuinely new world each cycle, which none of these games have to handle simultaneously.

## How notable games solved this

### Elden Ring / Dark Souls: carry-over done right, scaling done wrong

What carries over in Elden Ring NG+: character level, stats, equipment, key items, upgrade materials, boss weapons, Spirit Ashes, and (in the DLC) Scadutree Fragments/Shadow Blessings. What resets: NPC questlines, story/world state, and unlit Sites of Grace, though the map stays revealed ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/elden-ring-new-game-plus-guide-how-ng-works-and-what-carries-over/1100-6501400/), [Fextralife wiki](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/New+Game+Plus)). This is the right shape of carry-over/reset split for a procedural RPG: mechanical progress persists, narrative state resets so the story can be experienced meaningfully again.

The scaling, however, is the textbook cautionary tale. Enemies get flat HP/damage multipliers per NG+ tier, runes earned go up (550% by some tallies), and the whole thing caps at NG+7 where "all subsequent journeys will have an identical level of difficulty and rewards" ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/elden-ring-ng-plus-changes-rune-increase/)). Players explicitly call this out: at NG+7 the game "stops getting more difficult, which is a shame," and bosses get something as simple as a flat x1.4 HP multiplier ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3183488224885243285/)). Nothing new is asked of the player: no new boss attacks, no new enemy placements, no new world hazards. It is pure numbers-go-up, and it caps out because the designers correctly intuited that numbers-go-up scaling stops meaning anything past a certain multiplier, but they had no other lever to pull.

### Hades: modular, player-selected difficulty as the antidote to flat scaling

Hades' Pact of Punishment (unlocked after the first "true" ending) replaces flat NG+ scaling with 15 independent Heat modifiers the player selects and ranks individually, each contributing 1-5+ Heat to a running total that can theoretically reach 64 ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/10287-hades-pact-of-punishment-heat-modifiers-and-how-to-maximize-your-rewards), [Hades Wiki](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Pact_of_Punishment)). Crucially, most of these modifiers are not "enemies have more HP." Examples: Damage Control gives every enemy/boss a shield that must be broken before real damage lands (a new interaction, not a bigger number); Forced Overtime increases enemy attack/move speed by a flat 20% per rank (a tempo change); other conditions add extra enemy waves, remove healing fountains, or add environmental hazards. Only "Hard Labor" is a pure damage multiplier, and even that is capped and explicit about its rank-by-rank value (20% at rank 1, up to 100% at rank 5).

The reward loop ties directly to Heat: bosses killed at or above a target Heat drop Diamonds, Ambrosia, or Titan Blood, meta-currencies used for cosmetic/relationship content, not raw power, which avoids a "get stronger to fight things that are stronger because you got stronger" treadmill. The player, not the game, decides how much harder each run is, and they decide via mechanic selection rather than a single slider.

### Slay the Spire (1 and 2): stacking mechanical modifiers instead of a number

Ascension levels are additive: Ascension 3 has the effects of Ascension 1 and 2 plus its own ([Fandom wiki](https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Ascension)). Only some levels are pure numeric nerfs; a large share are structural: more Elites spawn on the map (a frequency/exposure change), enemies get new intents or attack patterns at high Ascension, bosses get entirely new attacks. Slay the Spire 2 pushes further by combining what used to be two separate Ascension levels into one and by building "many encounters around specific counters rather than raw stat checks," i.e., new required player behavior, not bigger monster stat blocks ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/slay-the-spire-2/ascension-levels), [AllThingsHow](https://allthings.how/slay-the-spire-2-ascension-explained-every-level-and-how-it-differs-from-the-original/)). The scoring system also grants a flat percentage bonus per Ascension (5% per level, capping at 100% at Ascension 20), giving a legible extrinsic reward for opting into harder modifiers, independent of whether a given run is won.

### Dead Cells: Boss Stem Cells as a discrete, biome-aware difficulty ladder

Boss Stem Cells (0-5) are earned by beating a boss with fewer active, then unlocked at the next tier. Each additional cell increases enemy damage/HP/aggression, but also changes enemy *composition*: new enemy types appear, existing enemies get new abilities, elites become more frequent, and specific biomes gain new hazards or sub-bosses ([Gamepur](https://www.gamepur.com/guides/dead-cells-boss-stem-cells), [Medium](https://medium.com/@tunganh0806/difficulty-as-design-dead-cells-progressive-challenge-and-player-engagement-74f086064bf6)). Healing is also throttled at higher cell counts, which is a resource-economy change, not a monster stat change. This is architecturally close to what Aegis needs: a discrete ladder where each rung reshapes the encounter pool and economy of a run, on top of (not instead of) a procedurally regenerated layout.

### Diablo 3/4 and Path of Exile: the "infinite number" endgame and why it needs a decoupled meta-currency

Diablo 3 has 16 Torment tiers (loot/XP multipliers, no new mechanics) plus Greater Rifts, an unbounded, separately-scaling ladder where monster HP/damage/XP increase by a fixed factor per level and difficulty is otherwise irrelevant to it ([Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/d3/resources/greater-rift-explained), [PureDiablo](https://www.purediablo.com/gameinfo/greater-rifts-diablo-3)). Diablo 4's Paragon board is the more interesting piece: it is an *unbounded but structurally gated* power-growth system layered on top of a level cap, where Glyphs (nodes that boost nearby board regions) level up via a separate currency (Pit Tiers) rather than raw playtime, and the studio has repeatedly rebalanced the "how much flat % damage should this unbounded system contribute" knob because letting it be the primary damage source made all preceding difficulty content trivial ([Wowhead](https://www.wowhead.com/diablo-4/guide/gameplay/paragon-boards-nodes-glyphs), [Boostmatch](https://boostmatch.gg/blog/diablo-4/articles/diablo-4-season-13-lord-of-hatred-paragon-guide)). The lesson: an "infinite" progression system needs its own gate (a tier you must clear to unlock the next slice of it) or it either does nothing (too slow) or trivializes everything else (too fast).

Path of Exile's Atlas passive tree is the strongest "endgame as a build-your-own-difficulty-and-reward-curve" example: map modifiers simultaneously raise monster difficulty *and* pack size/rarity/quantity, and the Atlas tree lets players intentionally specialize into specific encounter types (breaches, delves, etc.) rather than a single global slider ([Maxroll](https://maxroll.gg/poe/getting-started/atlas-progression-guide), [PoE Vault](https://www.poe-vault.com/guides/atlas-passive-skill-tree-strategies)). This is risk/reward as a *choice space*, not a single number, which is exactly the Hades philosophy applied to an ARPG endgame.

### Rogue Legacy: the closest existing analog to "carry a lineage into a freshly generated world"

Rogue Legacy's NG+ keeps all castle upgrades permanently, regenerates a new castle each run (or lets the player lock a favorite layout), and scales gold/rune value up to 10x while increasing enemy level, density, and room complexity ([Rogue Legacy Wiki](https://roguelegacy.wiki.gg/wiki/New_Game_Plus)). Its core conceit, playing a new heir each life rather than the same immortal character, is a soft narrative reset baked into the premise: death is not a failure state to explain away, it is the mechanism that justifies the world and challenge regenerating. This is worth studying for Aegis even though Aegis's death is not permanent at the individual-run level; the *NG+ transition* in Aegis is structurally similar to Rogue Legacy's "next heir enters a new castle," and Rogue Legacy shows that a straightforward multiplicative economy scale (up to a cap) is tolerable for currency-like carryover so long as the *encounter design* (room complexity, enemy variety) is also scaling, not just the numbers.

### Grim Dawn: shrines/devotion as a "second currency" gate on late-game power

Grim Dawn's Devotion/Celestial Power system requires shrines that, at Ultimate difficulty, demand rarer materials to activate, and Celestial Powers only level up while actively slotted and in use, capping per tier (Tier 1 caps at level 20, Tier 3 at level 10) ([Grim Dawn Wiki](https://grimdawn.fandom.com/wiki/Devotion)). This is a good pattern for "a system that keeps growing but resists just being farmed to infinity": growth is gated behind active use and a hard per-node cap, forcing horizontal expansion (more nodes/constellations) rather than one node scaling forever.

### Risk of Rain 2: what happens when scaling truly has no ceiling and no design intervention

This is the most important cautionary/pitfall finding. RoR2's Directors accumulate spawn "credits" that grow linearly with a difficulty coefficient over time, and higher-difficulty enemies have spawn-cost minimums. As runs go long, the difficulty coefficient grows into the thousands, but enemy stat/cost design does not scale to match: cheap enemies stop being spawnable (they're "too cheap" relative to the credit pool), tougher enemies aren't numerous enough to fill the credit budget, and stages can end up completely empty, with even scripted teleporter bosses failing to spawn ([Steam guide: The Math Breakdown](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2200625979), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/632360/discussions/0/3759975793256456839/)). This is a direct, concrete example of what "infinite-scaling math" failure looks like in practice: a formula that scales one variable (difficulty coefficient) without a matching scaling of the content it is supposed to drive (spawnable enemy variety at that cost band) breaks down completely, not just gets stale.

### Incremental games: prestige as designed psychology, not just numeric reset

Prestige loops (Cookie Clicker-style) reset raw progress in exchange for a permanent multiplier or new currency, and are explicitly designed so the *post-reset early game feels fast and empowering* rather than punishing: previously slow stages clear quickly, giving a legible sense of "I am now stronger than I was" ([Missions Zanx](https://missionszanx.com/guides/progression-and-scaling-in-incremental-games)). The commonly cited implementation detail is a diminishing-returns exponent (roughly 0.5-0.8) applied to the prestige currency's effect, so that resetting remains net-positive but each successive prestige yields proportionally less than the last, which is exactly what keeps players resetting repeatedly instead of resetting once and then grinding forever, or never resetting because it feels bad ([Medium: Clicker Games technical exploration](https://medium.com/@tommcfly2025/clicker-games-a-technical-exploration-of-incremental-system-architecture-b6d842e6963e)). The core psychological insight transfers directly to NG+: *the first hour of a new cycle must feel like a visible, legible power step up*, even if the long-run math is a diminishing-returns curve.

### Roguelike self-imposed challenge (NetHack conducts, DCSS)

NetHack's "conducts" (pacifist, vegan, foodless, etc.) and DCSS's cosplay/weekly challenges are optional self-imposed restriction layers tracked and scored, rather than developer-imposed difficulty multipliers ([DCSS Tournaments](https://dungeoncrawlstonesoup.com/Tournaments)). They're a weaker fit for Aegis's needs (they add restriction, not new threats), but they validate the general principle that a long-lived roguelike community sustains interest via *player-authored constraint variety* rather than the base game continually escalating numbers, an argument for giving Aegis players optional, chosen "sigils/covenants" akin to Hades' Pact rather than a single forced NG+ tier ladder.

## Pitfalls and failure modes (synthesized)

1. **Flat multiplier scaling caps out and telegraphs its own irrelevance.** Elden Ring's designers themselves cap the multiplier at NG+7 because higher would be either meaningless or would break other systems (item scaling, poise, status thresholds). A numbers-only approach has an inherent ceiling; plan for it rather than being surprised by it.
2. **Decoupled "infinite" systems can silently break content they're supposed to gate**, as in Risk of Rain 2's empty late-game stages. Any Aegis system with an unbounded scalar variable (a "world hostility" score, say) needs a corresponding *content* scaling plan (new enemy types/mechanics unlocked at each band), not just a formula, or the world will run out of things able to represent that scalar.
3. **Power creep that outpaces content trivializes the low tiers of NG+**, exactly the concern named in the assignment: an endgame Diablo 4 Paragon build can approach 1,000-1,500% Overpower damage, meaning early-game/NG+1-equivalent content becomes a non-event unless the game actively re-golds the encounter design each tier ([Wowhead Paragon guide](https://www.wowhead.com/diablo-4/guide/gameplay/paragon-boards-nodes-glyphs)).
4. **Resetting the wrong things breaks the "authored-feeling narrative" pillar.** Elden Ring resets NPC/quest state on NG+ specifically so story content can be experienced again; a game that instead makes quests permanently "done forever" starves an infinite-NG+ loop of its narrative content within a few cycles. Since Aegis regenerates the *entire world*, this problem is inverted and easier (a new world means genuinely new NPCs/quests), but it puts pressure on the world generator to make each cycle's storylines feel non-repetitive in structure, not just in dressing.
5. **A single global difficulty slider produces a mediocre-for-everyone result**, whereas Hades/PoE/Slay the Spire's modular, opt-in composition of specific modifiers lets each player build the specific flavor of "harder" they find fun (more tactical vs. more attrition vs. more time pressure), which also gives designers many small, independently tunable knobs instead of one knob that has to be right for every player at once.
6. **Currency/loot-only rewards for higher difficulty create a pure power spiral with no ceiling**, whereas Hades deliberately ties Heat rewards to *cosmetic/relationship/completion* currencies rather than raw combat power, which stops the reward loop from feeding the power-creep problem in #3.

## Recommendations for Aegis

1. **Split carry-over into three explicit buckets and commit to them in code/data schema now: Character (always full carry: stats, skills, equipped/owned items, known recipes/lore), Meta-progression (a bounded, diminishing-returns currency/unlock track, prestige-style, that is deliberately smaller in effect than raw character power), and World (always fully regenerated: map, NPCs, factions, quests, dungeons).** This mirrors Elden Ring's stat/equipment carry-over plus reset narrative state, but goes further by generating a genuinely new world rather than resetting flags in an old one, which sidesteps Elden Ring's "identical NG+7" staleness by construction since the content itself is new, not just harder.

2. **Do not scale primarily through a flat enemy stat multiplier. Scale through a discrete "World Hostility Tier" (analogous to Dead Cells' Boss Stem Cells / DCSS's tension) that unlocks new enemy types, new environmental hazards, and new AI behaviors at each rung, with only a modest (not compounding-forever) stat multiplier attached.** Concretely: NG+1 might introduce roaming elite patrols and one new enemy family; NG+3 might add a world-hostility mechanic like corrupted weather zones or NPCs who can now permanently die and be lost; NG+5+ might add faction-level threats (a standing hostile army) that didn't exist at NG+0. This directly avoids the Elden Ring cap-out failure and the RoR2 "credits with nothing to spend them on" failure, because each tier's difficulty is backed by designed content, not a formula alone.

3. **Let the player compose difficulty rather than assign it a single number, Hades-style.** Give Aegis players a set of independently rankable "world sigils" or "covenants" at NG+ transition (harsher weather, smarter enemy factions, scarcer resources, permadeath-for-companions-only, faster world corruption, etc.), each contributing to a visible "Threat" score, with better loot/currency tied to Threat achieved rather than to raw NG+ number. This solves both the "one-size-fits-all slider is mediocre" pitfall and gives you dozens of small, independently tunable balance levers instead of one multiplier that has to be right for 10+ cycles.

4. **Cap the primary meta-progression currency's power contribution and gate its growth behind active engagement, Grim Dawn/Diablo 4 Paragon-style, and apply a diminishing-returns curve (roughly the 0.5-0.8 exponent from incremental-game design) to whatever "prestige" bonus persists across worlds.** This keeps an overpowered NG+5 character from trivializing NG+1-equivalent threats purely via a bottomless meta-currency, while still giving the psychologically important "I feel stronger immediately" jolt at the start of each new cycle that makes players want to prestige again (this is the single most load-bearing lesson from incremental-game design: the reset must feel good in the first ten minutes, not just be justifiable on a spreadsheet).

5. **Treat "world hostility" as a first-class generation parameter, not a post-hoc enemy-stat multiplier, so it can never decouple from content the way it did in Risk of Rain 2.** Concretely, the world generator itself should read the NG+ tier and decide *what kind of world* to build: more hostile factions, more dangerous terrain types, higher base threat level of procedurally placed dungeons, rather than a combat-layer script scaling monster HP after the fact. This keeps escalation legible in the fiction (a harder world genuinely looks and plays differently) and guarantees content exists to back up any hostility number the design calls for.

6. **Reserve permanent, irreversible narrative consequences for the meta-layer between worlds, not within a single world's death/reload loop.** Since death is never permanent within a playthrough (a core pillar) but the world itself is discarded and regenerated at NG+ transition, that transition is the natural place to let truly consequential, semi-permanent story beats land (a companion who does not return in the next world, a title/reputation that persists as flavor, a "hall of legends" record of past-world deeds), giving the infinite loop emotional throughline without contradicting the no-permadeath promise.
