# Magic Systems in a Classless RPG: Research and Recommendations

## Overview

Aegis is classless: seven attributes bought with Essence, ~18 use-based skills, perks at thresholds. Magic cannot be a separate progression track bolted on top of that (a "mage class" hiding inside a classless game). It has to be expressed through the same currencies as everything else: attributes gate access, skills grow through use, gear and consumables gate execution, and the world (not a level-up menu) hands out capability. This document surveys how existing games have solved (and broken) the pieces of that problem, then makes concrete calls for Aegis's Mind/Will axis.

The core tension in every system surveyed below is the same one Aegis faces: magic wants to be legible enough to plan around (Sanderson's "hard magic" for player-facing systems) while still feeling numinous and worldbuilt (soft magic for lore and NPC-facing magic). Games resolve this by splitting the difference: player spells are hard-ruled and priced, while the world's ambient magic (why it exists, what the Aegis is, what a "storm of the wound" looks like) stays soft and mysterious.

## How Notable Games Solved (and Struggled With) This

### FromSoftware: sorcery/incantation as gated equipment, not a class

Dark Souls and Elden Ring never give the player a "mage" flag. Anyone can cast, provided they meet the stat requirement and are holding the right catalyst. Sorceries need Intelligence and a staff; incantations need Faith and a seal (dragon incantations also want Arcane); both spend FP, a resource shared with weapon arts, not siloed to magic. [Sorceries](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Sorceries), [Incantations](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Incantations).

Two design choices matter for us:

1. **Spell slots decoupled from attribute investment.** In the old Souls games, Attunement slots came from a stat, so a "hybrid" build was punished twice (had to buy both Intelligence for power and Attunement for slots). Elden Ring cut that cord: base Memory Slots are fixed at 2, and additional slots come from finding **Memory Stones** in the open world, up to 8 total. Investing in Mind (their Intelligence analog) buys spell power and FP, not slot count. This means a Might/Wits hybrid loses nothing structural by picking up a utility spell here or there; the "how many spells can I carry" question is answered by exploration, not by a tax on the attribute that already gates power. [Memory Slots](https://eldenring.wiki.fextralife.com/Sorceries), [How to Get More Spell Slots](https://gamerant.com/elden-ring-get-more-spell-slots-how-extra-space-memory/).
2. **Acquisition is loot, not a menu.** Every spell is a physical item sold by an NPC, dropped by a boss, or sitting on a corpse in a cave. There is no "spend points, pick from list" screen. This makes magic feel like *world knowledge you found* rather than *a tab you unlocked*, and it naturally paces power: the strongest spells gate behind the same exploration risk as the strongest weapons.

Sorceries and incantations are also mechanically differentiated rather than being reskins of the same tree: sorceries cast faster and are more FP-efficient (offense/utility focus), incantations are pricier per-cast but include healing and buffs and reward standing still in melee range (support/tank-adjacent focus). That differentiation is what lets each stat (Int vs Faith) feel like a different play pattern rather than a different damage-number skin.

### Bethesda: Morrowind's spellcrafting broke on the exact axis it was proudest of

Morrowind let any character walk up to a spellmaker NPC and construct custom spells from a library of effects, costed by magnitude/duration/area. This is the most "magic as an authored crafting system" any AAA RPG has shipped, and it is instructive because of *how* it broke, not just that it did:

- Effects were priced independently but combined multiplicatively in value. Player-discovered combinations (e.g., a spell that stacks Fortify Skill effects, or one that drains-then-restores a skill to "reset" its training cost) produced arbitrarily powerful or exploitable results the cost formula never anticipated. [Spellmaking](https://elderscrolls.fandom.com/wiki/Spellmaking_(Morrowind)), community discussion of exploits confirms this was widely known and reproducible.
- The fix in Skyrim was not to repair the pricing model, it was to **delete spellcrafting entirely**. Skyrim mages are stuck with hand-authored spells only. This is broadly regarded by the community and by later "bring back spellmaking" retrospectives as a net loss for the mage fantasy, because it removed the one system that made a caster build feel authored by the player rather than picked from a fixed menu. [Elder Scrolls 6 Can Bring Back Morrowind's Best Feature](https://screenrant.com/elder-scrolls-6-morrowind-spellmaking-mage-gameplay-skyrim/).

The lesson isn't "never allow spell composition," it's: **any combinatorial spell-crafting system needs its cost function validated against combinations, not against individual effects in isolation**, and ideally needs a small closed set of composable primitives (not 100+ orthogonal effects) so the combinatorics stay auditable.

### Caves of Qud: mutations as a magic-analog with genuinely independent axes

Caves of Qud has no "magic stat." Mental mutations (telepathy, precognition, mental mirror, temporal fugue, etc.) sit on the same unlock system as physical mutations (regeneration, quills, wings) and are drawn from the same pool at chargen and via mutation-granting events, gated by mutation points, not by a dedicated int-like stat. Critically, a mental mutation's *effective level* scales with the character's Ego attribute bonus, and Ego-derived bonus levels can push a mutation past the normal level-10 cap. [Mutations](https://wiki.cavesofqud.com/wiki/Mutations).

This is a useful structural pattern distinct from FromSoft's: instead of "attribute gates access, item gates execution," Qud does "attribute scales a thing you already own." A player who invests in Ego makes their existing mental mutations better without that investment being wasted if they also want a strong body. Because physical and mental mutations draw from the same currency (mutation points) and can both be taken by any character, there is no "magic class" in Qud, just builds that lean mental, physical, or mixed, exactly the shape Aegis wants for Mind/Will vs Might/Grace/Vigor.

### Hard vs soft magic (Sanderson) applied to game systems

Sanderson's First Law: an author's ability to resolve conflict *with* magic is proportional to how well the audience understands the magic's rules. Translated to games: **anything the player is expected to plan around (their own spells, and enemy telegraphs) must be hard-ruled and legible; anything that exists purely for atmosphere or lore (the Aegis's nature, ambient world magic, NPC-only rituals) can and should stay soft.** [Guide to Sanderson's Laws](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/guide-to-sandersons-laws-of-magic-lecture-notes). Tabletop commentary echoes this split: hard magic suits player-facing powers (clear cost/effect so choices feel fair), soft magic suits antagonist/horror material where mystery is the point ([Legend Keeper: Hard and Soft Magic at the Table](https://www.legendkeeper.com/hard-and-soft-magic/)). Aegis's telegraph-clarity-scales-with-knowledge combat design is already a Sanderson-First-Law engine: the world's magic can stay mysterious in prose scenes and NPC lore, while the *combat-relevant* subset of it (what a telegraphed enemy cast will do) becomes hard-ruled precisely to the degree the player has earned the knowledge to read it.

### GURPS: magic as skills, not a separate mechanic (and its overload problem)

GURPS Magic treats every individual spell as its own skill, gated by prerequisite spells and (usually) a single governing attribute (IQ). This is the purest "magic as skill system" precedent: no mana-class abstraction, casting literally uses the same skill-roll mechanics as swinging a sword. [GURPS Magic Redesign](http://ottgaming.grimoire.ca/GURPS_Magic_Redesign). Its documented failure mode is relevant: with 400+ nominally independent spells, many spells ended up functioning as strictly-better substitutes for mundane skills (bypassing tool requirements, situational penalties, etc.), and all of that power funneled through one overloaded attribute (IQ), recreating a de facto "casters dominate" problem despite the skill-based framing. [GURPS Magic Redesign](http://ottgaming.grimoire.ca/GURPS_Magic_Redesign). The lesson: making magic "a skill like any other" only prevents class-like dominance if individual spells are actually costed against the mundane skill they'd replace, and if the governing attribute isn't allowed to become a silent tax-free win button.

RuneQuest/Glorantha's three parallel magic systems (Spirit, Rune, Sorcery) are worth noting for a different reason: they deliberately give magic **internal texture** rather than one undifferentiated "magic points" pool. Spirit magic is common, cheap, short-duration, drains a shared point pool. Rune magic is rare, sacrifices permanent power for potent effects (a hard opportunity cost, not just a resource spend). Sorcery is the flexible, composable tier where a caster manipulates range/duration/strength directly. [Magic in RuneQuest and Its Descendants](https://frank-mitchell.com/rpg/magic-in-d100/). This maps well onto a tiered "cantrip vs ritual vs signature-spell" structure rather than one flat spell list.

### Casting cost and risk models

The industry has converged on a handful of cost/risk currencies, each implying a different play pattern:

- **Mana/FP pool** (most JRPGs, Souls games): clean, fast, but degenerates into "wait and spam" unless regen is slow or combat-scarce. World of Warcraft's own history is the cautionary tale here: players could evade intended per-cast costs by "downranking" to cheaper spell versions, and Blizzard eventually had to force every rank of a spell to cost the same percentage of max mana specifically to kill this exploit, because, in their own framing, "costs are the single most important part of game balance, and being able to evade costs has always led to degenerate gameplay." Relevant summary: [Blessing of Kings: Downranking is Dead](https://blessingofkings.blogspot.com/2008/08/downranking-is-dead.html).
- **Stamina-shared** (Elden Ring's FP is separate from stamina, but many action games merge them): forces a choice between casting and dodging/attacking in the same turn, which is closer to what Aegis's grid combat wants.
- **Health/HP-as-cost** (blood magic archetypes): makes every cast a real wager, but needs a hard floor (can't-die-from-your-own-spell) or players stop engaging.
- **Sanity/corruption/moral-standing** (Divinity: Original Sin 2's Source Vampirism, Eternal Darkness's sanity effects, Symbaroum's corruption): the cost isn't paid in a resource bar, it's paid in reputation, a Villain tag, or a shifting worldstate. DOS2's Source Vampirism literally decrements a hidden "goodness" score and can flip a character's alignment tag on repeated use, tying a spell's cost directly into the game's reputation/quest-reactivity system rather than a meter. [Source Vampirism](https://divinityoriginalsin2.wiki.fextralife.com/Source+Vampirism).
- **Miscast/backlash tables** (Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay's Winds of Magic): casting is a gamble with a fail-forward table, not a binary success/fail. A failed roll doesn't just "fizzle," it rolls on a Miscast table with escalating minor/major/catastrophic entries, and players can spend reagents/ingredients at cast time specifically to downgrade a rolled miscast (major to minor, minor to none). [Miscast Table](https://8th.whfb.app/magic/miscast-table). This is a strong precedent for "reagents as risk mitigation" rather than "reagents as a hard cost gate."

### Ritual vs combat magic

Nearly every system that has both keeps them on separate economies: combat magic is priced for repeatability (mana/FP, fast to cast, small individual effect) while ritual magic is priced for rarity and stakes (long cast times measured in minutes/hours, consumed reagents, requires safety, i.e., not castable mid-fight, and produces large, often permanent, world-state changes: raising the dead, binding a spirit, warding a location). WFRP's Winds of Magic and RuneQuest's Rune magic (sacrifice permanent power for the spell) both encode this: the "big" magic costs something the character can't easily get back, which is precisely what makes it feel earned rather than spammable.

### Turn-based tactical casting: making it feel weighty

Final Fantasy Tactics's charge-time system is the classic example: many powerful spells have a multi-tick windup before they resolve, visible to every unit on the field, so the correct counterplay is to kill or interrupt the caster before the tick lands, not to out-heal the result. Discussion of the system explicitly notes the tension: making casts instant would erase the entire point of interrupt-based counterplay, so the windup *is* the mechanic, not a tax on it. [12 ways to improve turn-based RPG combat systems](https://sinisterdesign.net/12-ways-to-improve-turn-based-rpg-combat-systems/). This is the turn-based equivalent of FromSoft's telegraphed enemy wind-ups and maps directly onto Aegis's intent-telegraph grid: a spell should be a *visible commitment on the timeline*, not an instant menu action, so that spellcasting and melee both live inside the same "read the telegraph, decide to interrupt/dodge/block/counter" loop instead of magic being a way to skip that loop.

## Pitfalls and Failure Modes (Cross-Cutting)

1. **Slot-tax double dipping.** If the attribute that powers spells also gates how many you can carry, hybrid builds get penalized twice and casters get railroaded into over-investing in one stat. (Old Souls Attunement vs Elden Ring Memory Stones.)
2. **Combinatorial spellcrafting without combinatorial cost validation.** Any "compose your own spell" system needs its pricing tested against combinations, not just individual effects, or it becomes an exploit engine (Morrowind), which then invites the worse fix of deleting the feature outright (Skyrim).
3. **Overloaded single governing attribute.** If every spell in a large list keys off one stat with no differentiation, that stat becomes a silent win-button and the "magic is just a skill" framing stops meaning anything (GURPS Magic's documented critique).
4. **Cost evasion loops.** Any tiered or rank-based spell cost needs the cheap rank to be truly weaker in impact, not just cheaper per use, or players will farm the cheap version indefinitely (WoW downranking).
5. **Undifferentiated resource pools flatten playstyle.** A single "mana bar" spent identically by every spell removes the texture that makes different magic traditions feel different (contrast with RuneQuest's three-tier system or WFRP's per-Lore Winds).
6. **Instant-cast magic breaks telegraph-based combat.** If a spell can resolve with zero visible windup, it either has to be drastically weaker than a telegraphed action or it silently invalidates the read-and-react loop the rest of combat is built on.
7. **Magic that's mechanically a class in disguise.** Any design where the "correct" way to play a caster is to dump everything into one attribute and one skill line re-creates a class, just without the label. The antidote demonstrated by Qud is to make the magic-relevant attribute a *multiplier on owned capability* rather than a *gate on total capability*, so partial investment still pays off.

## Recommendations for Aegis

1. **Split casting access from casting power, the way Elden Ring split Memory Slots from Intelligence.** Mind and Will should gate *how strong and how reliable* a spell is (damage/effect magnitude, success odds, backlash odds), never *how many spells you can carry or cast per encounter*. Spell "slots" (call them Bound Words, Sigils, whatever fits Aegis's lexicon) should come from world objects (shrines, mentor training, a found grimoire page) so a Might-primary character who picks up two utility spells along the way loses nothing structural and doesn't need to half-build into Mind to make them usable. This is also the direct enabler of the "every activity must feed at least two of skill growth/money/reputation/story hooks" pillar: acquiring a slot becomes a world event, not a menu unlock.

2. **Gate spell acquisition entirely through diegetic sources, never a level-up list.** Concretely: grimoire pages as lootable/quest-reward items (parallel to Elden Ring's sorcery/incantation items), mentor NPCs who teach a spell after a quest or relationship threshold (feeds reputation/story hooks per the activity-breadth pillar), and shrines/ritual sites tied to world facts from the causal-grammar worldgen (a spell literally traces to a Fact the way every quest must). This keeps magic's pacing under the same authorial control as gear and quest content instead of a separate XP-gated tree, and it means NG+ regeneration can vary *which* spells exist in a given cycle's world, which a fixed level-up list cannot do.

3. **Cost magic on the same resource axis as martial actions (Stamina/turn economy), with Mind/Will scaling risk, not the base resource pool.** Do not give casters a segregated "mana bar" that martial builds never touch; that's the single fastest way to smuggle a class back into a classless game. Instead: casting consumes the same per-turn action/stamina economy everyone budgets, and Mind/Will determine *miscast odds and backlash severity* (WFRP-style), with reagents as an optional cost paid at cast time to downgrade a bad roll rather than a hard cast-blocking requirement. This keeps "how many actions can I take this fight" identical across builds while still making high-Mind characters meaningfully more reliable casters, exactly the independent-axis, composable-build requirement locked into the design.

4. **Make every spell a visible, interruptible commitment on the intent-telegraph grid, with a tiered cast-time structure: instant utility cantrips (weak, telegraph-light, usable defensively) vs multi-tick signature spells (strong, telegraphed early and interruptible, akin to Final Fantasy Tactics charge time and FromSoft boss wind-ups).** This guarantees magic plugs into the same read-and-react combat loop as martial telegraphs instead of offering a way to bypass it, and it gives Wits/knowledge investment (telegraph clarity scaling) a natural payoff against enemy casters specifically.

5. **If Aegis wants any spell-composition or crafting layer (a grimoire-annotation, rune-combination, or ritual-design system) resembling Morrowind's spellmaking, keep the primitive set small (roughly 6 to 10 orthogonal effect verbs) and cost-validate pairwise/triple combinations explicitly, not just individually.** Treat it the way RuneQuest treats Sorcery, as the flexible, expensive, rare tier sitting above a fixed set of common "spirit magic"-style cantrips and mentor-taught set-pieces, rather than as the primary acquisition path. This gets the player-authorship payoff Elder Scrolls fans still miss from Morrowind without reproducing its exploit surface, and it gives high-Mind/high-Will characters a build-defining lever that low-investment casters simply don't have access to (asymmetric payoff for specialization, without magic ever becoming mandatory).

6. **Reserve true soft magic (undefined rules, no player lever) for world-facts and the Aegis's own nature, never for anything the player casts or must counter in combat.** Per Sanderson's First Law, anything the player is expected to solve a problem *with* must be legible; anything meant to generate mystery and dread (why the Aegis resurrects you, what a Wound-tier hazard actually is before the player has the knowledge threshold to read it) can stay soft, prose-only, and outside the spell list entirely. This lets the fiction keep its numinous Elden-Ring-esque opacity while the player-facing spell system stays a fair, plannable, hard-ruled subsystem.
